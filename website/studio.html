Three planning studies

Each pair tackles a different part of the same question. What can Kajicho retain, where does it need to adapt, and how can more people use it?

Climate Resilience and Green Space

Regina Aura Delazenitha uses vegetation and surface-temperature mapping to locate gaps in greenery. Keane Carlo G. Lomibao tests how roadside planting beds could retain stormwater in a sewer model.

73.8%
Of the satellite study area falls in the two lowest vegetation-index classes.

Connect small shaded places along streets and beneath the railway. Where runoff collects, test planted spaces that can hold water.

Transportation and Perceptual Cohesion

Shair Muhammad examines railway crossings and designs space for cycling on two Kajicho streets. Dingchao Shi maps how it feels to walk, what is open at street level and where people can pause or meet.

The aim is to improve connections and make everyday public spaces more comfortable for leisure and social activity.

What makes a place worth pausing in?

The team looked for five visible cues from street imagery. A place to sit, shade, greenery, an edge to lean against or stand by, and an open shop.

The map records visible features in sampled images. It does not count people or measure how long they stay.
