# Local rules and implementation

Source findings, local interpretation and proposed design responses ·2026-10-15

## Capacity depends on the street

**Source finding:** The reviewed ward zones are commercial, with designated FAR of 600% or 800%, a base building coverage ratio of 80%, and fire-zone designation. Article 43 of the Building Standards Act generally requires at least 2 m of legal-road frontage; Articles 52, 53, 56 and 61 govern FAR, coverage, height and fire performance. Road width and a site spanning zones affect permitted floor area.

**Local interpretation:** Of 26 flagged building footprints already checked, 25 cross the official 600%/800% boundary and one lies in a single zone. Street frontage and block interiors therefore need separate site checks. A footprint is not a legal site boundary. The mapped FAR is a starting condition; it does not establish how much any one owner can build.

**Design response:** For each candidate site, show use, designated FAR, legal road width, frontage, effective FAR, setbacks, fire conditions and the height envelope. Compare retrofit and ground-floor improvements alongside feasible redevelopment. Confirm fire-related exceptions and site conditions before using capacity in a design option.

**Scope:** Kajicho 1–2; legal sites and roads require individual confirmation

**Still to check:** Legal site boundaries, certified road widths and individual approval records are missing.

## Nearby plans protect homes and lanes

**Source finding:** The 4.5 ha Kanda Konyacho plan combines housing retention, mixed uses and setback space for walking. The 14.8 ha Naka-Kanda Chuo plan northwest of Kanda Station promotes homes above shops and offices while retaining lanes and a continuous street edge. Its named area includes Kanda-Kajicho 3.

**Local interpretation:** Kajicho 1–2 and Kanda-Kajicho 3 are distinct areas. The plans provide nearby evidence of the tension between office growth, residential continuity and small-building renewal. They inform the design question of keeping local services and walkable lanes. Their 40/45 m height limits and setback rules apply only within the corresponding mapped districts.

**Design response:** Present these as adjacent-area precedents with clear scope. Map ground-floor use, opening hours, homes above, narrow-lane access and renewal sites. Test whether a proposal keeps everyday services and walking links connected.

**Scope:** Adjacent Konyacho and northwest Kanda; not applied as core-area rules

**Still to check:** Boundary-edge sites require the official plan drawings and legal site boundaries.

## Energy, roofs and flood protection

**Source finding:** Chiyoda requires prior environmental consultation for qualifying new or extended/rebuilt parts of at least 300 m². Its effort targets are BEI ≤0.65 for non-residential uses and, provisionally, ≤0.80 for housing. BEI compares design primary energy with the standard value. Manual v14 also covers flood-safe electrical equipment, water barriers, vegetation and roof/surface treatments.

**Local interpretation:** Kajicho needs separate records for measured operating energy, design performance after retrofit and consultation requirements for construction. Roof treatment, shade and equipment renewal can be compared together, with attention to indoor loads, street radiation and where equipment can remain usable during flooding.

**Design response:** Show use and area, the meter/proxy source, candidate measures, BEI applicability and owner/operator for each building. Check subsurface conditions and maintenance for rain gardens, and material performance and reflected radiation for cool roofs. Keep design targets separate from observed operating savings.

**Scope:** Qualifying building works in Chiyoda; check use and filing route

**Still to check:** Bills, ownership and equipment records are missing; BEI is not a measured saving rate.

## Read walking as everyday use

**Source finding:** The 2022 walking strategy assesses sitting, eating, shade and evening lighting alongside walking, rest and conversation. It differentiates pedestrian-only, pedestrian-priority, shared and vehicle-priority streets. Pilot locations should combine local partners, road functions and useful street-level places.

**Local interpretation:** Proximity to Kanda Station does not describe how a street works. Routes to workplaces, lunch venues and community places need short-stay spaces. Assess morning through-movement, lunchtime use and evening social activity separately, then match the street section to those uses.

**Design response:** Add a morning/noon/evening table beside the map: through-flow, staying and duration, clear width, loading/parking, seats, shade and active ground floors. Select a short segment with local partners and workable access, then document the before/after use.

**Scope:** Ward policy applied through Kajicho street observations

**Still to check:** Time-of-day movement, staying, clear width and shade need field observations.

## What Kajicho has already tried

**Source finding:** The published 2025 Kajini 2 report records an October 16–17 parking-lane trial on Kanda Station East Exit Street. It describes lunch and evening use, but also narrow sidewalks, parking and difficult entry. Reported income and spending were ¥1.2 million: ¥0.3 million from the ward and ¥0.9 million from the committee.

**Local interpretation:** This gives the studio an existing local network: a university, development/design/operation partners, the town association and watage. The 2026 ward page also lists Kajini 4 on Kamishirakabebashi Street linking Kajicho 2 and Uchikanda 3. The east exit–underpass–west exit sequence is therefore a concrete survey route.

**Design response:** Check current conditions and follow-up records, then compare passing through with choosing to stay. Develop shade, visible entry, a clear walking line, furniture storage and daily operation together. The team can contribute timed observations and heat-exposure records, with permits, security and communication shown in the same delivery plan.

**Scope:** 2025: Kajicho 2-7-1–7-15 and 2-8-1–8-14; 2026 listing: Kamishirakabebashi Street

**Still to check:** The 2025 account is an organiser report, without a controlled long-term estimate. The 2026 listing supplies a programme, not verified outcomes.

## Give each action an operator and budget

**Source finding:** The 2023 guide assigns a role to residents, workers, shops and businesses and explains public-space procedures. In 2026 Chiyoda introduced a trial recognition scheme with coordination and operational support. It assesses public benefit, need, effect, delivery, continuity and collaboration. Applications for that year are closed.

**Local interpretation:** Shade, seating, rainwater features and shared energy all need daily operators and funding. A road installation can involve both the road manager and police; food service has its own procedure. The guide’s clear-width conditions belong to the relevant road-occupation case and must be checked against the actual street.

**Design response:** Each action should name the land/facility owner, operator, approval contacts, funding, hours, cleaning/storage and review record. Build a draft role plan through interviews with existing local groups and places such as watage. Treat future support as a funding option until awarded.

**Scope:** Chiyoda; approvals depend on the street, land and activity

**Still to check:** Partner consent, road ownership and eligibility for future funding remain to be checked.

## Follow flood risk to entrances and services

**Source finding:** Chiyoda’s March 2025 Kanda map cites 2018/2021 inundation forecasts and 2024 river-flood data, with 690 mm total and 153 mm peak hourly rain. Depth and duration are separate. Arakawa breach and storm surge have different scenarios. The building manual calls for flood-aware electrical equipment, entrance protection and occupant preparation.

**Local interpretation:** Label the core-area GIS map as the 2018 Kanda inundation forecast and keep the 2024 river layer within its own model scope. Differences between their coloured areas do not establish a risk trend. Basement access, low electrical equipment and movement routes connect mapped flooding to interruption of daily services.

**Design response:** Show scenario, edition, depth classes, model coverage and building intersections beside the map. Field-check thresholds, basement entries, barriers, electrical equipment, safe upper-level access and operators. Rain gardens and storage are design options to size and test; they do not automatically reduce official hazard classes.

**Scope:** Separate Kanda inundation, river-flood, Arakawa and storm-surge scenarios

**Still to check:** Entrance and equipment levels need field data; no drainage model or flood recalculation was run.

## Both chome need a collapse-risk view

**Source finding:** Tokyo’s ninth survey, released in September 2022, assigns both Kajicho chome collapse rank 3 of 5, with risk quantities of 4.15 and 3.81 buildings/ha. Fire ranks are 2 and 1; both composite ranks are 2. It compares 5,192 areas under equal bedrock shaking and includes emergency-response difficulty.

**Local interpretation:** A composite rank alone hides the collapse-related design question. Building retention/renewal and emergency access should be read together, including older buildings, façades/signs, narrow roads and obstructions. Area ranks are neither individual structural diagnoses nor earthquake probabilities.

**Design response:** Place a two-chome table for collapse, fire and composite values beside the flood map. For each design option, record retention/retrofit reasons, clear emergency paths, gathering spaces and responsibility for service continuity. Use owner records or professional surveys for building-level seismic status.

**Scope:** Kajicho 1–2; 2022 area-level assessment

**Still to check:** Building seismic records, present obstructions and emergency-organisation interviews are missing; record changes since 2022 separately.
